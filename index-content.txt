SECTION 01 — THE BEGINNING
================================================================

More than just a success story.

Walt Disney is remembered as an extraordinarily successful and
creative animator, filmmaker and theme-park developer.

But his success did not come easily. His journey was shaped by an
unhappy childhood, business failures, rejection and countless
setbacks.


================================================================
SECTION 02 — EARLY LIFE
================================================================

A difficult childhood

Disney was born in Chicago in 1901 and raised in Missouri.
His childhood experiences helped shape his imagination and
determination.

1901 — Born in Chicago
Walt Disney was born in 1901 and was the fourth son among five
siblings.

FAMILY — A Difficult Home
His father, Elias, was described as a domineering and abusive
figure. Disney often found escape through drawing.

1919 — A New Direction
After being rejected from the Army for being underage, he joined
the Red Cross and went to France as an ambulance driver.


================================================================
SECTION 03 — THE STRUGGLE
================================================================

Failure came early.

Before Mickey Mouse and Disneyland, Disney had already experienced
major disappointments.

TIMELINE
--------

1920 — Laugh-O-Gram Studios
Disney and his brother Roy launched their own cartoon business.
Only a few years later, the company went bankrupt.

AFTER BANKRUPTCY — Los Angeles
Disney tried acting but failed at that too. Instead of giving up,
he moved toward animation and convinced Roy to join him.

OSWALD — He lost his own character
Oswald the Lucky Rabbit became successful, but Disney discovered
that he had lost the legal rights to the character.

1928 — A new beginning
Rather than fighting over Oswald, Disney walked away and started
again. On the journey back to California, the idea that became
Mickey Mouse was born.


================================================================
SECTION 04 — THE TURNING POINT
================================================================

From a lost character to Mickey Mouse.

After years of struggle, Disney brought Mickey Mouse to life on
film in the late 1920s.

But even Mickey faced rejection. Investors rejected the famous
mouse more than 300 times before one finally accepted the idea.

STATS
-----
300+        — Investor rejections
1928        — Mickey Mouse era begins
1937        — Snow White released


================================================================
SECTION 05 — SUCCESS WASN'T EASY
================================================================

Even success brought problems.

Disney's growing business still faced financial, creative and
personal challenges.

FINANCIAL — Heavy Pressure
Disney often struggled financially and had to deal with growing
debts.

1937–1942 — Risky Films
Snow White became a huge success, but films such as Fantasia and
Bambi initially failed financially.

WORLD WAR II — Another Setback
Animators went on strike and the company struggled to rebuild
after the war.


================================================================
SECTION 06 — THE BIG GAMBLE
================================================================

Disneyland

Disney diversified into television and used the success of his
shows to raise enough capital for his biggest venture yet.

July 17, 1955

Disneyland opened in Anaheim, California.

But the opening was chaotic. Forged tickets brought huge crowds,
temperatures reached around 100°F, drinking fountains were not
working properly and some rides malfunctioned.

Critics called it "Black Sunday." Yet Disney persevered, and
Disneyland eventually became a colossal success.

SECTION 07 — THE LESSON
================================================================

What can we learn from Disney?

01 — Failure isn't the end
Bankruptcy, rejection and failed projects did not stop Disney
from trying again.

02 — Adapt to change
Disney moved from cartoons to films, television and eventually
theme parks.

03 — Keep moving forward
When one idea failed, Disney searched for another path instead
of staying stuck in the past.
